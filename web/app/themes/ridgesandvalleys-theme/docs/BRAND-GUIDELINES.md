# Ridges & Valleys Studio — Brand Guidelines

> This is the configured brand voice and style guide for the marketing plugin.
> Skills load this automatically. Apply it to every draft, review, campaign, and report
> unless the user explicitly asks for something different.

## Who we are

Ridges & Valleys Studio is a web design and local growth studio serving Gettysburg, Adams
County, and the wider South Central Pennsylvania region. We build fast, accessible WordPress
websites, lay local SEO foundations, and keep sites healthy after launch.

- **Website:** ridgesandvalleys.com
- **Descriptor:** Gettysburg & South Central PA web design and local growth
- **Tagline:** Built for the long view.
- **Secondary message:** AI-assisted. Human-finished. Locally supported.
- **Signature line:** AI handles the blank page. Matt handles the judgment.

The studio is new; the expertise is not. Matt has 15+ years of web development experience
(WordPress, Power Platform, accessibility-first front-end work). Never position the studio as
beginner or portfolio-building. Position it as a new company run by an established practitioner.

## The metaphor (what "Ridges & Valleys" means)

The name is the landscape of South Central PA — rolling ridgelines and open valleys. Lean on
that: **the long view, knowing the territory, high ground, mapping the route, built to last.**
Retire the old "root/rooted/growth" language entirely. We help a local business see the whole
landscape of its online presence and take the high ground.

## Positioning statement

Ridges & Valleys Studio builds fast, accessible WordPress websites for Gettysburg, Adams
County, and South Central Pennsylvania businesses. We use AI to accelerate research and first
drafts, then apply experienced human judgment to design, content, accessibility, and launch.

The critical distinction is **AI-assisted, not "cheap AI websites."** Always frame AI as a
speed and research accelerant, never as a replacement for human craft or judgment.

## Brand voice

Ridges & Valleys sounds like a knowledgeable local neighbor who happens to be a serious
professional. Warm, plainspoken, and credible — never salesy, never jargon-heavy, never
boastful.

Voice attributes on a spectrum:

- **Confident, not arrogant** — we know our craft and our region; we don't oversell or trash competitors.
- **Plain, not dumbed-down** — clear everyday language; explain value, not technology.
- **Warm, not folksy-gimmicky** — genuinely local, but polished and modern.
- **Outcome-first, not feature-first** — lead with what the business gains.
- **Honest, not hype** — accurate claims, realistic timelines, no overpromising.

### Tone by context

- **Cold outreach / audits:** helpful and observational. Open with a sincere positive note and
  one specific, useful improvement. Never "your website is bad."
- **Sales pages / proposals:** confident and reassuring; emphasize clear scope, speed, and local support.
- **Care/support communication:** calm, dependable, low-drama.
- **Social / community posts:** neighborly and generous — teach one small useful thing.

## Messaging pillars

1. **Local — built here, supported here.** A real person in the region, not a faceless agency
   or an overseas template shop.
2. **Fast without flimsy.** First working draft in ~3 business days; target launch in 7–10.
   Speed comes from a disciplined process, not corner-cutting.
3. **Accessible and correct.** Accessibility-first, human-fact-checked, built to last.
4. **AI-assisted, human-finished.** AI drafts; Matt decides, verifies, and approves.
5. **Clear scope, no mystery.** Fixed packages, one revision round, honest pricing.
6. **The long view.** Websites and local SEO foundations built to keep working, not just to launch.

## Preferred language

- Say **"website" / "site,"** "local SEO foundations," "Google Business Profile," "landing
  page," "care plan," "first working draft," "launch," "handoff," "the long view," "the territory."
- Use **"we"/"the studio"** for company voice; **"Matt"** when founder-led credibility matters
  (About, audits, the "Matt handles the judgment" line).
- Refer to the delivery process as **Groundwork to Launch.**
- Products/tiers: **Website Rescue, Local Launch, Growth Site, Care & Grow, Founding Local Launch.**

## Language to avoid

- The old metaphor: **"root," "rooted," "grow-as-a-plant"** framing — retired with the old name.
- "Cheap," "budget," "AI-generated website," "template," "guru," "ninja," "rockstar."
- Beginner framing: "new freelancer," "building my portfolio," "trying to get started."
- Tech-first jargon in customer-facing copy: Gutenberg, Sage, Tailwind, "AI models," plugin
  names. Lead with outcomes; mention tech only when a technical buyer asks.
- Overpromising: guaranteed rankings, "#1 on Google," unlimited revisions, "done overnight."

## Claims discipline (review flag)

- Do not claim a business is "currently looking for a website." Use observable opportunity
  signals only ("your tourism listing points visitors to Facebook").
- Verify every fact before it ships (hours, prices, addresses, product claims).
- Disclose that AI may assist with drafts while a human reviews all work.
- Never put confidential client information into a third-party model without permission.

## Target audience

Small, owner-operated Gettysburg / Adams County / South Central PA businesses — retail shops,
restaurants, galleries, inns, and event venues. Decision-maker is usually the owner. They buy
outcomes and confidence, not technology. Speak to their customers' needs: easy-to-find hours
and directions, more calls and bookings, accurate menus/events, less dependence on Facebook.

## Visual identity (for content, decks, and social)

Rule: **Historic, not antique. Local, not touristy. Fast, not flimsy.**

Colors:

| Token | Hex | Use |
|-------|-----|-----|
| Ink | #17151F | Headlines, footer, dark sections |
| Paper | #FFF9F5 | Main background |
| Ridge Green | #355C4D | Primary brand color |
| Gettysburg Brick | #A64B3C | Warm accent / secondary CTA |
| Harvest | #D7A542 | Highlights, badges (use dark text on it) |
| Iris | #6C4CF1 | AI / process / technology accents |
| Hairline Lavender | #ECE6FB | Borders, card dividers |

Ridgeline stripe: a thin bar reading **Ridge Green → Iris → Brick → Harvest** across page and
card tops — a stylized ridge-and-valley horizon.

Typography:

- **Outfit** — navigation, body, buttons, structural headlines.
- **Instrument Serif (italic)** — exactly one meaningful word in a major headline.
- **JetBrains Mono** — small labels, e.g. `GETTYSBURG · WEB DESIGN · LOCAL GROWTH`.

Avoid in imagery: cannons, battlefield silhouettes, flags as identity, parchment, Old-West/
Civil-War display fonts, heavy red-white-blue, generic neon "digital agency" looks. Prefer:
rolling ridgelines and valleys, brick storefronts, Lincoln Square geometry, the town clock,
open fields, warm evening storefront light, painted signs, Matt's own regional photography.

## Logo concept

A simple ridgeline mark:

- Two or three rolling strokes rise to a **ridge** and dip into a **valley**.
- The **ampersand (&) nests in the valley** between the ridges — the low point that joins them.
- The negative space of the valley can suggest a soft "V."
- The icon sits inside a softly rounded square.
- The full wordmark uses **Outfit**, not a historical script; style the "&" as the signature detail.

Use a one-color version first. The ridgeline gradient (Ridge Green → Iris → Brick → Harvest)
can be reserved for digital use.
